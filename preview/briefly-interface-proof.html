Sources
≤ 30 words The summary is assembled from ranked clauses by a word budget, then refused by a database constraint if it is still over. The counter runs live on the card you are looking at: —.
Confidence Confirmed means several independent outlets, or an official source, state it directly. Reported means one account, not yet corroborated. Disputed means sources contradict each other — and the card says so on its face rather than averaging them.
Source count Ten reports of one event become one story with ten sources, not ten near-identical cards. The clustering behind this is the single hardest part of the backend.
Diversity Three bars counting where reporting came from — how many independent newsrooms, how many kinds of outlet, how many countries. Not a bias score, and the caveat travels with the data so the number cannot be shown without it.
No imagery Briefly has no licence to a publisher's photography, so the card is type only. It loads instantly and holds up at every Dynamic Type size — a constraint that turned into the identity.
Serif / sans A serif headline against a sans body is what makes a card read as an edited page rather than a feed item.

What this proof does and does not show

Real

  • The layout, palette, type scale and copy are the ones in the SwiftUI source.
  • The story content is read from the JSON the app bundles, produced by the real backend pipeline — clustered, fact-extracted and word-budgeted.
  • The word counts are computed here, live, by the same rule the backend applies.
  • Every source list, diversity breakdown and confidence level is what the pipeline actually assigned.

Not real

  • This is HTML, not the compiled app. Gesture physics, momentum, Dynamic Type and VoiceOver behave differently on device.
  • The events, outlets, people, places and figures are invented for testing. Ordelia and Verland are not countries; Cyclone Verath did not happen.
  • Audio is not playing here. The real app ships two generated episodes and plays them offline.
